The blood pressure log your doctor asked for

Measure with your own cuff, and GamLog keeps the notebook: morning and evening readings, a one-page report for the clinic, and notebooks for the people you look after.

Tell me when it’s out See what it does

GamLog doesn’t measure blood pressure and doesn’t diagnose. Use your own cuff, then tell Gami the numbers.

Only the features you need

When a doctor or an Apple Watch says “take it twice a day for a week and keep a log”, GamLog is the notebook.

  • Two readings, morning and night

    Big, simple entry for each sitting. GamLog groups readings into morning and evening sessions and averages them the way clinics ask.

  • A one-page report for the clinic

    Seven days on one page, in black and white: every reading counted, averages with counts, your medicines and notes, and the guideline’s home reference with its source.

  • See what changed after a new dose

    Mark the day a medicine started, stopped or changed, and see it on the chart right next to your readings.

  • Keep the whole family’s log

    A notebook for you, a parent or a partner, each with its own readings, medicines and reminders.

  • Gentle reminders

    One nudge each morning and evening. If it’s already logged, Gami stays quiet.

  • Private by design

    No account, no ads, no analytics. Your readings stay on your iPhone, and Apple Health is only used if you turn it on.

A look inside

Meet Gami 감이

Gami is a small persimmon who stamps each reading into your notebook. Gami cheers you on for logging, never for your numbers: a reading of 118/76 and one of 158/98 get the same happy stamp.

The name comes from Korean: 감 (gam) is a persimmon, and 록 (rok), as in 기록, means a record. GamLog is in English and Korean from day one, with AHA/ACC 2025 or Korean Society of Hypertension 2026 categories.
